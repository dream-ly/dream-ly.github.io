I am a Human-Computer Interaction and Human-centered AI researcher studying future socio-technical systems. My work examines how emerging computational systems, especially AI agents, enter human life not only as tools, but as participants in everyday practices, relationships, and social organization. I also explore how designed systems can serve as probes into possible futures, making emerging forms of computational agency and human–technology interaction tangible and open to inquiry.

At the level of the self, I study how computational systems shape self-understanding, identity, memory, human agency, and personal narratives. At the relational and social levels, I examine how they transform connection, support, intimacy, trust, responsibility, cooperation, and the protocols through which people coordinate and sustain social life. I investigate these questions across selfhood, domestic, relational, posthumous, and institutional futures, through research on AI afterlife, family technologies, personalized behavioral interventions, AI-assisted decision-making, and distance communication.

I approach research through empirical, speculative, and systems-oriented HCI. Drawing on theories from sociology, psychology, and behavior, I combine qualitative and quantitative inquiry with the design of interactive systems. Rather than treating future technologies as distant abstractions, I use speculation, design artifacts, and real-world deployments to surface social, ethical, and relational tensions and to examine how emerging systems might reorganize everyday life.

I received interdisciplinary training in technology and design, which grounds my work in HCI and Human-centered AI. I am currently pursuing my Master of Science, advised by Prof. Carman Neustaedter at the School of Interactive Arts and Technology, Simon Fraser University. Previously, I earned my B.Eng. in Computer Science, East China Normal University with the highest honors.

Research Focus

Future socio-technical systems for better self-understanding, social life, and human-AI relationships.

  • Self-understanding, identity, memory, agency, and personal narratives
  • Connection, support, intimacy, trust, cooperation, and emerging social protocols
  • Selfhood, domestic, relational, posthumous, and institutional futures
