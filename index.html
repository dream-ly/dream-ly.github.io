Hi, this is Ying. I am a student researcher working at the intersection of Human-Computer Interaction, Human-centered AI, and Interaction Design.

I see myself, on the one hand, as a technology-oriented researcher. I am deeply interested in emerging Human-AI relationships, particularly how AI can augment human behavior and cognition, such as in AI-assisted decision-making and behavior intervention. I am also fascinated by how generative AI reshapes notions of self identity and relationships, for example in contexts like AI-enabled digital immortality.

On the other hand, I approach my work with a strong humanistic lens. I am interested in how people make sense of themselves and their relationships within complex life contexts. This includes studying family dynamics in situations such as migration, aging, divorce, and distance, where technologies are deeply entangled with emotional, social, and cultural experiences.

Across my work, I aim to bridge these two perspectives. I combine theory-driven approaches from psychology, sociology, and related fields with both qualitative and quantitative methods to design socio-technical systems that are not only technically innovative, but also socially meaningful, emotionally aware, and ethically grounded. My work is ultimately motivated by enhancing individual well-being and supporting real-world human needs through the design of emerging technologies.

I received interdisciplinary training in technology and design, which grounds my work in HCI and Human-centered AI. I am currently pursuing my Master of Science, advised by Prof. Carman Neustaedter at the School of Interactive Arts and Technology, Simon Fraser University. Previously, I earned my B.Eng. in Computer Science, East China Normal University with the highest honors.
